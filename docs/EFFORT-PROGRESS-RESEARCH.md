# Effort and performance signals — evidence and implementation choices

## Effort language

The primary 2016 study by Zourdos and colleagues describes a resistance-training-specific RPE scale linked to repetitions in reserve. The [AUT institutional repository abstract](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/items/efef3b25-6701-4fb5-bb82-55fcd2a26027) was opened and read: its mapping includes RPE 10 for zero repetitions in reserve and RPE 9 for one. It studied 29 experienced/novice squatters; its practical value does not establish that every beginner can estimate remaining repetitions exactly for every exercise.

The app asks how many additional reps the user thinks they could have performed with the same controlled form: No more, 1 more, 2 more, 3 more, or 4+ more. These are optional subjective estimates, not a test requiring the user to attempt failure. Standard approximate labels RPE10/9/8/7/6-or-less are secondary explanation only. The open-ended 4+ category must not be converted into a precise RPE6 claim. Missing effort is unknown, not zero reserve, and clearing a rating removes it rather than inventing an answer.

A secondary review's search snippet appeared to shift the mapping by one. We relied on the primary abstract's explicit 10-to-zero mapping rather than that snippet. No full-text appraisal is claimed.

## Why progress is useful but not a direct muscle-size measurement

[ACSM's 2026 review abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/) discusses strength, hypertrophy and other outcomes as distinct measures. The [2021 load review](https://journals.lww.com/acsm-msse/fulltext/2021/06000/resistance_training_load_effects_on_muscle.12.aspx) describes load-specific strength improvements involving both neural and skeletal adaptations. Search abstract evidence supports distinguishing logged performance from measured muscle size; this app does not measure muscle tissue.

The user wants a motivating muscle-oriented progressive-overload signal. Show grouped evidence of improved exercise performance, with actual before/after sets, dates and setup. Use “Performance improving” or counts such as “1 of 2 comparable exercises improved”; do not assert muscle growth percentage, verified hypertrophy or anatomy heatmap growth.

For a conservative clear signal compare the latest two finished sessions for an exact movement/gym/equipment/unit identity, with complete equal intended work-set counts and unchanged rep prescription. Corresponding sets must not decrease in either load or reps and at least one must increase. Greater tonnage alone, additional sets, mixed heavier/fewer-rep tradeoffs and partial sessions do not establish this signal. Known higher effort qualifies/suppresses the unqualified improvement. Missing effort is disclosed and never guessed. Assistance and bodyweight load semantics require separate interpretation, so generic load-progress claims are withheld there.

This deliberately leaves genuine but harder-to-interpret progress as reviewable data rather than assigning a false negative or “not growing” label. It is an observational product rule, not a validated hypertrophy prediction model or a prescription to increase every session.
